# Creative Mastery: Product Requirements (MVP)

**Promise:** Don't just learn the craft. Prove you can do it.
**Outcome:** measurable competence, not course completion.

## Personas
| Persona | Needs | Platform response |
|---|---|---|
| Complete beginner | No assumed knowledge, guided examples | Beginner path from first lesson, guided practice |
| Developing creative | Fix shaky fundamentals, explain decisions | Diagnostic placement (rigorous), targeted remediation |
| Aspiring professional | Portfolio, ambiguous briefs, rationale | Capstones, rubric-graded projects, portfolio |

All personas meet the same mastery standard. Placement may waive prerequisites only through assessed evidence.

## MVP journeys
1. **Enter:** sign up, pick a goal and discipline, choose starting level (or take diagnostic), land on dashboard.
2. **Learn:** open the next unlocked lesson, work through the lesson loop, pass the knowledge check (80%).
3. **Practise:** submit the practice task or assignment, with rationale.
4. **Be assessed:** receive rubric-based evaluation (criterion scores, evidence, actionable feedback, pass/fail).
5. **Recover:** on failure, get targeted remediation and retry within the resubmission rules, or request human review.
6. **Progress:** pass module assessment, then the capstone (85%), then the next level unlocks.
7. **Show:** publish a project to the portfolio with learner-controlled visibility; receive peer feedback.

## MVP scope
In: auth and onboarding, discovery, curriculum delivery, prerequisite enforcement, knowledge checks, assignments and private file submissions, AI rubric assessment, capstones, progression gates, resubmission, dashboard and timeline, basic skill profile, shareable projects with privacy, basic peer feedback, content versioning, server-side access control. Admin CMS follows once the content format is proven.

Disciplines in MVP: Graphic Design, Brand Design, UI Design (with a UX foundation). **First build** proves the loop with Graphic Design / Beginner / Visual Foundations / Contrast; the other paths are added as content.

Out for now: native apps, live classes, mentor marketplace, paid certificates, community feeds, leaderboards, tool integrations, advanced recommendation models, other disciplines.

## Binding rules (brief section 18)
1. Watching is not mastery. 2. No prerequisite bypass from the client. 3. Progression rests on assessment evidence. 4. Every assessment has a learning objective. 5. Every graded project has a published rubric. 6. AI feedback is specific, actionable and evidence-grounded. 7. AI states when it cannot inspect a submission. 8. Mandatory competencies are not offset by other scores. 9. Learners see why they passed or failed. 10. Failure leads to targeted remediation. 11. Peer feedback never replaces assessment. 12. Learner controls visibility. 13. Content is separate from code. 14. Shared foundations are reusable. 15. Historical results stay interpretable after rubric changes. 16. UI always shows the next meaningful action. 17. Competence over gamification. 18. No credential implies professional competence without supporting evidence.

## Open product questions
- Placement/diagnostic: which prerequisites can be waived and by what score?
- Resubmission limits and cool-down per assessment type.
- Human review: who reviews (staff, contracted experts) and SLA.
- Video hosting (start: uploaded MP4 + captions) and licensing of real-world example media.
- Certificate wording and whether any credential is issued in MVP.
- Disclosure rules for AI-assisted learner work.

## Success metrics
Learning: diagnostic-to-final gain, first-attempt and eventual capstone pass rates, retention in later assessments. Quality: AI vs expert agreement, appeal rate, repeat-evaluation variance, cost per submission, a11y defects. Do not optimise for time on platform.
