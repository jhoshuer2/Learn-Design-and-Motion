## {objective} Where we are going
By the end you can:

- name the three common formats and their shapes;
- re-lay out an animation for each format instead of stretching it;
- keep timing and easing identical so all three feel like one piece.

## {concept} The idea in one minute
<<video:one-motion-three-formats|The same three words in a square, a vertical story and a wide banner. Stretching squashes the letters, re-laying out keeps them clean, safe zones keep words clear of app buttons, and timing stays identical. Captions explain each step.>>

Posts come in different shapes: **square (1:1)**, **vertical story (9:16)** and **wide banner (16:9)**. Do not stretch one version to fit. **Re-lay it out** for each shape: new positions and sizes, same words. Keep the **timing, easing and stagger identical** so it still feels like one animation. On a story, keep words inside the **safe zone**: apps cover the top and bottom edges.

## {realWorld} Where you have seen this
Brands post one idea everywhere: a square on the feed, a tall version in stories, a wide one on a website. When it is done well you recognise it at once, even though every layout is different.

## {goodVsBad} Spot the difference
- **Stretched:** the same picture pulled to fit, letters squashed or stretched.
- **Re-laid out:** words moved and resized for the shape, letters untouched.

Same timing and easing in both. Only the layout changes.

## {guided} Try it with me
1. Start from your finished loop.
2. Duplicate the project and change the canvas to 9:16, then to 16:9 (CapCut: ratio option; Canva: resize; Figma: new frame).
3. Move and resize the words so each fits its shape. Keep letters at their normal proportions.
4. On the vertical one, keep the words away from the top and bottom edges.
5. Check that the delay between letters and the easing are the same in all three.
