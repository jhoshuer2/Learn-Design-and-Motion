## {objective} Where we are going
By the end you can:

- explain what a keyframe is in one sentence;
- animate position, scale and opacity together;
- ease your keyframes.

## {concept} The idea in one minute
<<video:keyframes-move-scale-fade|A square animates while a mock timeline shows the keyframe diamonds for position, scale and opacity. The captions explain each step.>>

A **keyframe** saves a value at a moment in time. You need **two** or more: the tool fills in the frames between them. Each property (position, scale, opacity) has its own keyframes. Add an easing and the move starts and stops gently.

## {realWorld} Where you have seen this
Every title that slides in and fades, every logo that pops, every app card that appears has keyframes under it. You are learning the same three controls professionals use.

## {goodVsBad} Spot the difference
- **Plain:** only position changes, at constant speed.
- **Richer:** position, a little scale up and a fade, all eased.

Three small changes, one smooth entrance.

## {guided} Try it with me
1. Add a shape or a word.
2. At 0 seconds set: position a bit left, scale 60%, opacity 0%. Add a keyframe for each.
3. At about 1 second set: position centre, scale 100%, opacity 100%.
4. Apply ease in-out or ease out to the keyframes.
5. Play it. If it feels stiff, try a slightly longer duration.

## {feedback} What next
