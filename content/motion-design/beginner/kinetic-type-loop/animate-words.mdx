## {objective} Where we are going
By the end you can:

- stagger letters or words so they arrive like a wave;
- reveal text with a mask;
- give every line time to be read.

## {concept} The idea in one minute
<<video:animate-words|The same word appears all at once, then with a letter stagger, then with a mask reveal, then in reading order with holds. Captions explain each.>>

A **stagger** starts each letter (or word) a tiny bit after the previous one, about **0.05 to 0.1 s** apart. A **mask** uncovers text like a curtain. Whatever you use, bring words in **reading order** and **hold** each line long enough to read: roughly a quarter of a second per word plus a beat.

## {demo} Play with it
Change the delay and see how the wave changes.

## {realWorld} Where you have seen this
Opening titles, lyric videos, social posts and ads all move words this way. When it works, you read the message without noticing the animation.

## {goodVsBad} Spot the difference
- **Hard to read:** letters fly in very fast and leave straight away.
- **Easy to read:** a short stagger in, a clear hold, then out.

If you have to rush to read it, it is too fast.

## {guided} Try it with me
1. Type a short word such as FRESH.
2. Split it into one layer per letter, or use the tool's text animation with a letter delay (CapCut: text animation In; Canva: Animate, Rise or Pop; After Effects: Animator with a Range selector).
3. Give each letter a delay of about 0.08 s.
4. Add a 1 second hold, then an out animation.
5. Watch it three times. Could a stranger read it?

## {check} Quick check
Answer these. You need 80% to complete the lesson. If you miss one, the explanation points you back to the part to revisit. No pressure: you can retry as often as you like.

## {task} Make a small thing
Animate three words in reading order with a stagger, a hold and an exit. Export a 5-second clip. Practice only, no grade.

## {reflection} How did that feel?
Where did you want to speed up? Where did you need more time? Both instincts are useful.

## {feedback} What next
If the words feel rushed, add hold time before changing anything else. Next: making it loop.
