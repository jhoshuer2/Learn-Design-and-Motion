## {objective} Where we are going
By the end of this lesson you can:

- tell a mechanical move from one that feels alive;
- pick a duration that suits the mood;
- add easing so a move starts and stops gently.

You do not need any experience. We start with one ball.

## {concept} The idea in one minute
<<video:motion-feels-alive|Two balls travel the same distance. One moves at a constant speed and one eases. The captions explain spacing and timing.>>

Motion feels alive when speed **changes** along the way. **Easing** is that change: slow start, faster middle, slow end. **Timing** is how long the whole move takes. Short (about 0.25 s) feels snappy. Around 0.7 s feels friendly. Over 1.5 s feels heavy.

## {demo} Play with it
Watch the three balls. Notice which one you would trust to open a door.

## {realWorld} Where you have felt this
A phone screen that slides up smoothly feels expensive. A lift door that moves at one speed feels cheap. You already know the difference in your body. Now you can name it.

## {goodVsBad} Spot the difference
- **Mechanical:** one constant speed from start to finish, stops dead.
- **Alive:** slows near the start and the end, quick in the middle.

Same distance, same time. Only the spacing changes.

## {guided} Try it with me
Use any free tool. Steps differ by app, the idea does not.

1. Add a circle or a square.
2. Make it move from left to right over **1 second** (in CapCut: add a keyframe at the start, move the playhead, move the shape; in Canva: Animate, then a Position or Slide effect; in Figma: two frames and Smart Animate).
3. Switch the easing from linear to **ease in-out** (CapCut: keyframe curve or easing option; Figma: Smart Animate easing; After Effects: select the keyframes and press F9).
4. Play both. Which one would you rather watch?

## {task} Make a small thing
Export two 3-second clips of the same move: one linear, one eased. Then try the eased move at 0.25 s, 0.7 s and 1.6 s. Write one sentence about how each one feels. This is practice: you will not be graded on it.

## {reflection} How did that feel?
Which duration felt most like you? There is no wrong answer. Noticing what you like is how taste grows.
