## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- explain why a brand is more than a logo;
- state a brand's position and audience in two sentences;
- point to the design choices (colour, type, shape, imagery, tone) that express that position;
- spot a design that contradicts the brand it is meant to serve.

## {concept} A brand is what people expect and remember
A **brand** is the set of expectations and feelings people hold about an organisation, product or person. It is built from many experiences: how a shop looks, how staff speak, how a package opens, how an app behaves.

A **logo** is one small part of that: a sign people use to recognise the brand. A **visual identity** is the system of design choices (logo, colour, type, imagery, layout, tone of voice) that makes the brand recognisable and consistent.

Designers work from a **position**: who the brand is for, what it promises, and how it is different from alternatives. A useful way to write one is two sentences: *"For [audience], [brand] is the [category] that [promise], unlike [alternative]."* Every later choice is tested against it.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. A design decision is good for a brand when it is **right for the position and the audience**, not when it is merely attractive.
2. A brand is built by **consistency over time**. One strong piece does not make an identity.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Think of two coffee places on the same street. One has chalkboards, mismatched mugs and a handwritten menu. The other has a pale interior, a short menu in small capitals and a single typeface. Both may sell equally good coffee. Each tells you who it is for and what to expect before you taste anything.

Now think of a local council website and a music festival website. One needs to be calm, plain and trusted by everyone. The other can be loud and playful. The same design moves (bright colour, tight tracking, big photography) would be wrong in one and right in the other.

## {guided} Try it with support
Pick a small local business you know. Work through these steps:

1. Write its position in two sentences using the pattern above. If you must guess, say so.
2. List the audience in a few words (who, and what they are trying to do).
3. Find three visible choices (colour, type, imagery, wording) and write what each says about the brand.
4. Decide whether each choice fits the position. Mark one that fits and one that does not.

Keep your notes. You will use them in the independent task.
