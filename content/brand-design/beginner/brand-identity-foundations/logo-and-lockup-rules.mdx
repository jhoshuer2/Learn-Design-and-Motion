## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- name the parts of a lockup (mark, name, and the space between them);
- set clearspace around a logo and a sensible minimum size;
- choose a version and a background that keep the logo legible;
- recognise and fix common logo misuse.

## {concept} A logo is used by rules, not by feel
A **logo** may be a symbol (a **mark**), a styled name (a **wordmark**), or both. A **lockup** is a fixed arrangement of those parts, for example the mark to the left of the name, or stacked above it. The relationship between the parts is set once and then repeated, never adjusted by eye.

Four rules keep a logo working:

1. **Clearspace.** Keep an empty margin around the logo, measured from something in the logo itself (for example the height of a letter), so nothing crowds it.
2. **Minimum size.** Below a certain size fine detail fills in or disappears. Set a minimum width and use a simpler version under it.
3. **Background and contrast.** Use a version with enough contrast against the background. Brands usually keep a full-colour version, a one-colour dark version and a one-colour light version.
4. **No alterations.** Do not stretch, rotate, recolour arbitrarily, add effects, or rearrange the lockup.

Rules like these are what let different people produce consistent work without asking the designer each time.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Look at a delivery van, a shop receipt and a small website icon for the same business. The van shows the full lockup with plenty of room. The receipt, printed small in one colour, uses a simplified single-colour version. The tiny icon uses the mark alone. One identity, three versions, each chosen for its use.

Brand guidelines often include a page of "do not" examples: stretched logos, logos on busy photographs, logos with drop shadows. Those exist because people keep making those mistakes.

## {guided} Try it with support
Take any logo you can open (a business near you will do). Work through these steps:

1. Draw a box around it and mark a clearspace margin equal to the height of one capital letter.
2. Shrink it until it stops being readable. Note the width at which that happens, then add a margin and call that the minimum size.
3. Place it on a dark background and a light one. Which version of the logo would you need for each?
4. List two ways someone could misuse it in a hurry, and write the rule that prevents each.

Keep your notes. You will use them in the independent task.
