## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- describe a brand voice in three or four traits, with an example line for each;
- write a headline and a caption that sound like the same brand;
- tell a specific line from a generic one;
- keep voice consistent across a headline, a caption and a label.

## {concept} Voice is how the brand sounds in words
A **brand voice** is the way a brand sounds when it writes. Like image style, it works best as a short list of traits with examples:

- **Traits**: for example warm, plain-spoken and a little dry.
- **Do and don't lines**: "The rye loaf is back on Fridays" (do), "We are pleased to announce a range of baked goods" (don't).
- **Habits**: short sentences, no capitals for shouting, no exclamation marks, a real detail in every line.

Voice sits next to the position and audience of the brand. A bakery for local families will not sound like a luxury hotel. **Specific** lines (a day, a flavour, a place) sound like someone; **generic** lines ("the best quality, great service") sound like nobody.

Headlines are short and lead; captions add one useful detail. Both should sound like the same person.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. Write traits you can test: could a line break this trait?
2. Prefer one real detail to three adjectives.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Compare the packaging of two coffee brands. One says "Premium quality, unbeatable taste." The other says "Roasted on Tuesdays. Best by the end of the month." The second sounds like people who make coffee.

Also notice voice in small places: an error message, a receipt line, a sign on a door.

## {goodVsBad} Compare these
**Headline.** Generic against specific.

- Generic: "Delicious baked goods for everyone."
- Specific: "Cardamom buns, Saturdays, while they last."

**Caption.** Stiff against warm.

- Stiff: "Our artisans prepare products using premium ingredients."
- Warm: "Baked at 5 a.m. so they are still warm when you collect them."

Why the specific versions work: they name something real, they are short, and they sound like the same plain-spoken person in a headline and in a caption.

2. Write three traits that describe how it sounds.
3. Write one *do* line and one *don't* line for each trait.
