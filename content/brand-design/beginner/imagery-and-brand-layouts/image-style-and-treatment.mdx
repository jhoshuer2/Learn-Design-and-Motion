## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- say what makes a set of images feel consistent (subject, light, colour, crop and treatment);
- write an image style as three or four rules a stranger could follow;
- crop and overlay images the same way every time;
- spot images that do not belong to a brand's style.

## {concept} A style is a set of repeatable choices
Images carry a brand as much as a logo does. A **brand image style** is a short list of choices that you make the same way each time:

- **Subject**: what is shown (the loaf, the hands, the counter) and what is not.
- **Light and colour**: warm or cool, soft or hard, bright or moody, and how saturated.
- **Crop and distance**: close and tight, or wide with space around the subject.
- **Treatment**: any filter, overlay or caption bar that is applied the same way.

A useful rule is specific and checkable: "warm natural light, the subject fills at least two thirds of the frame, no filters" can be checked. "Nice, authentic photos" cannot.

Keep **do** and **don't** examples. One image that breaks the rule shows the rule better than a paragraph.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. Consistency comes from repeated choices, not from similar subjects.
2. A rule you cannot check on a finished image is not yet a rule.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Look at a bakery, coffee shop or outdoor brand you know on social media. The best ones use the same light, crop and colour so that you can tell a post is theirs before you see the name.

Now think of a shop that posts whatever a phone captured that day. Each picture may be fine, but together they do not say who the shop is.

## {goodVsBad} Compare these
**Rules.** Vague against checkable.

- Vague: "Photos should feel warm and authentic."
- Checkable: "Natural window light, warm tones, the subject fills at least two thirds of the frame, no filters, one dark caption bar at the bottom when text is needed."

**Applying them.** An image that is cool and blue, shot from far away, with a heavy filter breaks all four rules. An image that is warm and tight, with the same caption bar, follows them.

Why the checkable version works: anyone, including a new team member, can look at a finished picture and say whether it follows the rules.

## {guided} Try it with support
1. Pick a brand you like and collect four of its images.
2. Write down what they share: subject, light and colour, crop, treatment.
3. Turn what you found into three or four checkable rules.
4. Say what kind of image would break each rule.

Keep your rules. You will use them in the independent task.
