## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- size and space tappable elements so a thumb can hit them reliably;
- list the states a component can be in and design each one;
- make states distinct with more than colour (shape, icon, text, weight);
- write an error message that says what went wrong and how to fix it.

## {concept} Make it easy to hit, and clear about its state
An interface is used with fingers, in motion, often one-handed. A tappable element therefore needs a **target** big enough to hit: guidance commonly recommends about **44 by 44 points** as a minimum, with **space between neighbouring targets** so a slip does not trigger the wrong one. The visible shape may be smaller than the target, but the tappable area should not be.

A component is rarely in one condition. Common **states** are:

- **default**: ready to use;
- **selected** or **active**: chosen;
- **focused**: the one receiving keyboard or assistive-technology input, shown by a visible outline;
- **disabled**: unavailable, and ideally explained;
- **error**: something needs fixing, shown with a message.

Do not signal a state with **colour alone**: some people cannot tell the colours apart, and screens get used in bright light. Add a second cue such as an icon, a shape change, a label or a heavier outline.

An **error message** should be specific, sit next to the field, and say how to fix it ("Enter a 6-digit code"), not just that something failed.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
On a phone you have probably mis-tapped a link that sat too close to another, or a tiny "close" icon on an advert. Those are small, crowded targets.

Think of a sign-up form that turns a field border red when something is wrong and says nothing else. A person who cannot see red has no idea what happened. A good form places a short message below the field, adds an icon, and keeps focus visible.

## {guided} Try it with support
Take any form or list screen you can open. Work through these steps:

1. Estimate the size of three tappable elements. Which is under 44 points in either direction?
2. Check the gap between neighbours. Would a thumb hit the wrong one?
3. List the states you can find (default, selected, disabled, error). Which depend on colour alone?
4. Rewrite one vague error message so it says what went wrong and how to fix it.

Keep your notes. You will use them in the independent task.
