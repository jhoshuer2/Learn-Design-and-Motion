## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- say what a screen is for, and what to do next, after two seconds of looking;
- use size, weight and value to rank information into levels;
- apply one spacing scale so that closeness shows what belongs together;
- remove or demote elements that compete with the one main action.

## {concept} Rank the information, then space it
A screen shows many things at once. **Visual hierarchy** is the ranking you give them, so a viewer knows what to read first, second and third and what to do.

Three tools build it:

- **Size and weight**: larger, heavier text reads as more important.
- **Value and colour**: strong contrast against the background draws the eye; quiet tones recede.
- **Position and space**: things near the top, with room around them, read first.

**Spacing** does a second job: **proximity groups things**. Items that sit close together read as one group; larger gaps read as separation. Using one **spacing scale** (for example 4, 8, 16, 24 and 32 points, built from multiples of 8) makes gaps deliberate, so the group structure is clear and the screen feels orderly.

On a phone there is little room, so a screen should usually have **one main action**. Secondary actions are quieter, and everything else supports the decision.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. If everything is emphasised, nothing is. Demote before you decorate.
2. Gaps are information. Use only a few sizes of gap and use them the same way every time.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Open a banking app or a transport app on your phone. Most have a large balance or route at the top, grouped rows of details beneath, and one filled button at the bottom. Settings screens group related switches under quiet headings with a bigger gap between groups.

Now think of a form where every field, label and note is the same size and evenly spaced. You can use it, but you have to read everything to learn the order.

## {guided} Try it with support
Take any screen you have open on your phone. Work through these steps:

1. Look for two seconds, then write what the screen is for and what you would do next.
2. List the levels of information (for example title, details, action) and the tool that ranks each (size, weight, value or position).
3. Measure or estimate three gaps. Are they from a small set of sizes?
4. Name one element that competes with the main action and say how you would demote it.

Keep your notes. You will use them in the independent task.
