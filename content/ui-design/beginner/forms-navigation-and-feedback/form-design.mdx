## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- put a visible label above every field instead of relying on placeholder text;
- group related fields and keep the order logical;
- mark optional fields and write a specific button label;
- spot forms that ask for too much or hide what they need.

## {concept} Make each field obvious and each step short
A form is a conversation: each field asks one question. People complete forms when the questions are **clear**, **few** and **in a sensible order**.

- **Visible labels.** Put a label above each field. A placeholder disappears when someone types, so it is a hint at most, never the only label.
- **Grouping.** Keep related fields together under a short heading (who is travelling, extras). Space between groups is larger than space inside them.
- **Only what you need.** Every extra field lowers the number of people who finish. Mark optional fields as optional and ask for the rest only when you must.
- **One column on a phone.** Fields stacked in one column are easier to scan and tap than fields side by side.
- **A specific action.** "Continue to payment" tells people what happens; "Submit" does not.

Fields must also be large enough to tap (about 44 by 44 points) and have room between them.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. If a person cannot tell what a field is for after they start typing, the label is missing.
2. Ask only for what you will use.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Think of a sign-up form on a gym or library website. The good ones put "Email" above the box and keep it there while you type. The frustrating ones show "Email" in grey inside the box, and when you click it vanishes and you cannot remember what the field was for.

## {goodVsBad} Compare these
**Labels.** Placeholder only against visible label.

- Placeholder only: the field says "Phone" until you type, then it says nothing.
- Visible label: "Phone number" stays above the field, and "(optional)" is shown if it is not needed.

**Button.** Vague against specific.

- Vague: "Submit".
- Specific: "Continue to payment".

Why the strong versions work: people can check what they entered, understand the structure at a glance, and know what the button will do before they press it.

## {guided} Try it with support
1. Pick a form you used recently (a booking, a sign-up or a contact form).
2. List every field. Mark each one as needed, nice to have, or not needed.
3. Group the needed ones and write a visible label for each.
4. Write a specific button label.

Keep your list. You will use it in the independent task.
