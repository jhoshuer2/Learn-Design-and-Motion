## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- tell people where they are, where they can go and how to go back;
- choose between a bottom bar and a simple back link for a small app;
- label navigation items with words, not only icons;
- mark the current item with more than colour.

## {concept} Where am I, where can I go, how do I get back
Navigation answers three questions on every screen: **Where am I? Where can I go? How do I get back?**

- **Where am I.** Mark the current item clearly. Use a second cue as well as colour, such as bold text or an underline.
- **Where can I go.** For three to five main areas on a phone, a **bottom bar** with short labels works well. Keep the same items in the same order on every screen.
- **How do I get back.** Inside a task such as a form, give a visible **back** link or button. Do not trap people.
- **Words as well as icons.** Icons alone are easy to misread. Pair them with a short label.

Navigation targets must meet the same size rule as any other control (about 44 by 44 points). Keep the number of main items small. If everything is in the bar, nothing stands out.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. Always show where you are, using more than colour.
2. Never leave a task without a visible way back.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Open a banking, shopping or transport app. The main areas sit in one bar at the bottom, the current area is highlighted and a back arrow appears whenever you go deeper.

Now think of a site where you land on a page from a search and cannot tell which section you are in or how to get to the home page.

## {goodVsBad} Compare these
**Current location.** Colour only against more than colour.

- Colour only: the current tab is a slightly different grey, which is invisible to some people.
- More than colour: the current tab is blue, bold and underlined.

**Labels.** Icons only against icons with words.

- Icons only: a circle, a triangle and a square.
- With labels: Tickets, Timetable, Account.

Why the clear versions work: nothing needs to be guessed or remembered.
