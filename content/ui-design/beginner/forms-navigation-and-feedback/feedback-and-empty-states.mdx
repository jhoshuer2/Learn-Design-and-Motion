## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- show that the app is working, has succeeded, or has failed;
- write an error message that says what is wrong and how to fix it;
- show an error with more than colour, next to the field it belongs to;
- design an empty screen that says what will appear and what to do next.

## {concept} Tell people what just happened and what to do next
Every action should get a response. Four kinds of feedback matter most:

- **Loading**: say that the app is working ("Saving your details…") and avoid a screen that seems frozen. Disable the button while it works so it cannot be pressed twice.
- **Success**: confirm what happened and what comes next.
- **Errors**: say **what is wrong** and **how to fix it**, next to the field, in words. Add an icon and an outline so the error is not shown by colour alone. "Error" is not a message; "Add the part after the dot, for example .com" is.
- **Empty states**: when a list has nothing yet, say what will appear here and what to do ("No tickets yet? Choose a departure to see them here.").

Errors should be polite and specific, keep what the person typed, and move focus to the first problem.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. An error message names the problem and the fix.
2. An empty screen is an instruction, not a blank.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Think of a checkout where a field turns red and nothing else happens, compared with one that says "Your card number should have 16 digits" next to the field. Or an inbox that shows only a white screen compared with one that says "No messages yet. New messages will appear here."

## {goodVsBad} Compare these
**Error text.** Vague against specific.

- Vague: "Error" or "Invalid input".
- Specific: "Add the part after the dot, for example .com".

**Cue.** Colour only against more than colour.

- Colour only: a red border.
- More than colour: red border plus a warning icon plus text.

**Empty.** Blank against guiding.

- Blank: nothing is shown.
- Guiding: "No tickets yet? Choose a departure to see them here."

Why the strong versions work: people always know what happened and what to do.

## {guided} Try it with support
1. Trigger an error in an app or website on purpose (a wrong email, an empty required field).
2. Write down what it said and whether you could fix it from the message alone.
3. Rewrite the message to name the problem and the fix.
4. Find an empty screen in an app and write a better one.

Keep your rewrites. You will use them in the independent task.
