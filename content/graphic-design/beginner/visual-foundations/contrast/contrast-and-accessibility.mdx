## {objective} What you will be able to do
- State the minimum ratios for normal text, large text and interface components;
- measure a pair and decide whether it passes;
- repair a failing pair without losing the design's character;
- explain why some people need these minimums.

## {concept} A ratio, not an opinion
The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) define contrast as a ratio between the relative luminance of two colours, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white).

At level AA the main minimums are:

- **Normal text:** 4.5:1
- **Large text** (roughly 24px regular, or 18.5px bold, and up): 3:1
- **Interface components and meaningful graphics** (for example the border of an input, or an icon that carries meaning): 3:1

Purely decorative elements and disabled controls are exempt. Level AAA asks for more: 7:1 for normal text.

These numbers exist because many people see less contrast than you do: low vision, ageing eyes, colour-vision differences, a dim screen, or sunlight on a phone. Meeting the minimum is a floor, not a target.

## {realWorld} Where designs fail
Common real-world failures: placeholder text in form fields set in pale grey; text on brand-coloured buttons where the brand colour is light (white on yellow is a classic); thin icon strokes in a light tone; and disabled-looking links that are actually active.

Teams check this in design tools and automated audits, but the audits only measure pairs they can see. A person still has to decide which text sits on gradients, images and states like hover and focus.

## {goodVsBad} Failing and passing, side by side
| Pair | Ratio | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Black on white | 21:1 | Passes everything |
| `#767676` on white | 4.54:1 | Passes AA for normal text |
| `#777777` on white | 4.48:1 | Fails AA for normal text (by a hair) |
| `#949494` on white | 3.03:1 | Passes only for large text and UI components |
| White on `#f2b705` (yellow) | 1.82:1 | Fails everything |
| `#1a1a1a` on `#f2b705` | 9.57:1 | Passes comfortably |

The yellow button shows the usual repair: keep the brand colour, change the text colour. Making the text bold does not change the ratio, so it does not fix the failure.

Never rely on colour alone to carry meaning, such as red for errors with no icon or text. Contrast and colour-independence are separate checks, and you need both.

## {guided} Try it with support
1. In the lab, set the background to `#f2b705` and the text to white. Read the verdict.
2. Change only the text colour until all AA lines pass. How dark did it need to be?
3. Set the text to `#767676` on white, then change one hex digit at a time. Find the exact step where it fails.
4. Decide: would you ship the passing pair for a 12px legal footnote? Why might you want more than the minimum?

This competency is treated as critical for accessibility. You need 80% to complete the lesson.

## {task} Independent task
Audit five text and background pairs from a real interface. Record the measured ratio of each, whether it passes AA for its size, and a fix for each failure. Do not use the example pairs from this lesson.

## {reflection} Reflect
Which failing pair surprised you most, and what did you expect before measuring? What does that tell you about judging contrast by eye?

## {feedback} What to practise next
If the threshold questions were shaky, rewrite the three AA numbers from memory until they stick. If the repair questions were shaky, repeat the yellow-button exercise with two other brand colours. After this module comes the module assessment, which is not built yet in this prototype.
