## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- point to where contrast is steering attention on a landing page, and explain why;
- say whether a button reads as the main action, and what makes it so;
- explain how contrast groups or separates blocks of information;
- diagnose a layout where everything competes equally.

## {concept} Contrast is difference, used on purpose
Contrast is a noticeable difference between two things. It can be a difference in **value** (light against dark), **size**, **colour**, **weight**, **shape**, **spacing** or **texture**.

The eye is drawn to difference. Where nothing differs, nothing leads, and the viewer has to work out the order themselves. So contrast is less a decoration than a way of deciding, in advance, **what is read first, second and third**.

Two rules to carry through this module:

1. Contrast is relative. A shape is only "strong" against something.
2. Contrast is limited. If everything is emphasised, nothing is.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Think of a typical software landing page. The headline is large and dark, supporting text is smaller and softer, and a single saturated button is the loudest colour on the page. That is a reading order built from three kinds of contrast at once: size, value and colour.

Now think of a settings screen. Section titles are slightly heavier, and each section sits on its own slightly lighter or darker surface. The contrast here is quiet, but it tells you where one group ends and the next begins.

Next time you open an app, squint until the detail blurs. What is still visible is where the designer put the contrast.

## {guided} Try it with support
Take any screen you have open. Work through these steps:

1. Squint or blur the page. Write down the first three things you notice.
2. For each, name the kind of contrast responsible (size, value, colour, weight, spacing).
3. Decide whether that is what the page *wanted* you to notice first.
4. Find one thing that should have been louder, and one that is louder than it deserves.

Keep your notes. You will use them in the independent task.
