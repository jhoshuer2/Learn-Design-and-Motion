## {objective} What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you can:

- choose a body size that suits the medium and reading distance;
- set a line length that is comfortable, and fix lines that are too long or too short;
- set line spacing (leading) that opens the text without breaking it apart;
- choose alignment that helps reading, and avoid awkward rags.

## {concept} Four settings decide whether text is comfortable
Readers do not notice good text settings, but they feel bad ones. Four settings matter most:

- **Size.** Body text on a printed A4 page is commonly about 10 to 12 points; on a phone it is commonly about 16 points. Text that is too small tires the eye.
- **Line length** (the *measure*). Around **45 to 75 characters** per line is comfortable. Longer lines make it hard to find the start of the next line; very short lines break the rhythm.
- **Line spacing** (*leading*). Roughly **1.3 to 1.6 times the type size** for body text. Tight leading makes lines collide; very loose leading lets the paragraph fall apart.
- **Alignment.** Left-aligned text with a ragged right edge is usually easiest to read. Centred text suits short lines (titles), not paragraphs. Justified text needs enough characters per line to avoid ugly gaps.

These settings work **together**: a larger size usually wants a longer line and more space between lines. Check them by looking at the text at the size it will really be read.

## {realWorld} Where you meet this every day
Open a newspaper column and a paperback. Both use short lines and generous leading so you can read for a long time. Now think of a website where text runs the full width of a wide screen: your eye loses its place at the end of each line.

A children's book may use larger type and more space. A legal notice may use smaller type, which is exactly why it is hard to read.

## {guided} Try it with support
Take a block of text from a page you can open. Work through these steps:

1. Count the characters on three typical lines. Where does the average fall against 45 to 75?
2. Estimate the type size and the line spacing. Is the spacing about 1.3 to 1.6 times the size?
3. Look at the alignment and the right edge. Is there an awkward rag or big gaps?
4. Change one setting and write down whether it helps.

Keep your notes. You will use them in the independent task.

## {task} Independent task
Set a paragraph of about 80 words in two ways: one that breaks at least two of the guidelines, and one that follows them. Show both as images, list the settings you used for each (size, line length in characters, line spacing, alignment), and explain in a few sentences which is easier to read and why.

## {reflection} Reflect
Which setting did you find hardest to judge by eye? How could you check it with a measurement instead?

## {feedback} What to practise next
If line length was hard to judge, count characters on five paragraphs. If spacing was unclear, compare the same text at 1.0, 1.4 and 1.8 times the size. Next lesson: organising a whole page on a grid.
