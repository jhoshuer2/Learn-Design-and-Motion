# Design one mobile screen

**Assignment objective:** Show that you can design a single mobile screen with clear hierarchy, consistent spacing, usable targets and states, and readable text, and explain your decisions with measurements.

**Scenario:** Tidewater Ferries runs a small passenger ferry between two towns. Its app needs one screen where a rider picks a departure. The screen shows the route (Harbour Quay to Northpoint), a date, three departure times with seats left (07:40 with 12 seats, 09:10 with 3 seats, 11:30 sold out), and one main action: continue to passenger details. The screen is for a phone about 390 by 844 points. It is fictional: nothing you make is used by a real operator.

## Requirements
- Establish a clear hierarchy: the route and date first, then the departures, then the one main action.
- Show all three departures, including the sold-out one in a clearly disabled state that does not rely on colour alone.
- Make every interactive element at least 44 by 44 points, with space between neighbouring targets.
- Use body text of at least 16 points and keep essential text at or above the accessibility contrast minimums.
- Use one spacing scale consistently (for example multiples of 8 points) and group related items.
- In your rationale explain three design decisions and give the measurements you used: target sizes, font sizes and at least two measured contrast ratios.

## Constraints
- Original work only. Any icon, illustration or typeface must be one you are licensed to use.
- Do not copy a real app's screen or a template.
- Use only the information in the brief. Do not invent extra features, fares or contact details.

## Deliverables
- Screen design (required): PNG, JPEG or WebP export of the full screen at phone size or larger.
- Small-scale preview (required): The same screen exported at about 400 px on its long side, to check that the hierarchy still reads at thumbnail size.
- Process image: A sketch or an earlier version, if you want to show your process.

## Rubric: Mobile screen design (pass mark 80%)

### hierarchy-and-layout: Hierarchy and layout (weight 20%)

Does the screen make the reading order obvious (route and date, then departures, then the one main action) and use layout to support it?
Strong (85-100): a viewer can say within two seconds what the screen is for and what to do next; levels of importance differ clearly in size, weight or value.
Adequate (60-84): the main idea leads, but two levels compete or the main action is not the most prominent.
Weak (0-59): several elements have similar emphasis, or the main action is hard to find.

### spacing-and-grouping: Spacing and grouping (weight 15%)

Is one spacing scale used consistently, and are related items grouped so that proximity shows what belongs together?
Strong (85-100): a consistent rhythm, clear groups, aligned edges. Adequate (60-84): mostly consistent, with some uneven gaps or loose alignment. Weak (0-59): arbitrary spacing, items that look grouped but are not, or the reverse.

### targets-and-states: Touch targets and states (weight 20%, REQUIRED minimum 60%)

Are interactive elements large enough (about 44 by 44 points) with room between them, and are states clear (the sold-out departure shown as disabled, selected versus unselected)? You cannot measure exact sizes from pixels: judge what is visible, say it is an estimate, and compare with the sizes the rationale reports.
Strong (85-100): all targets are comfortably sized and spaced; every state shown is distinct without relying on colour alone.
Adequate (60-84): targets are probably adequate but one is tight, or a state relies mostly on colour.
Weak (0-59): targets are visibly small or crowded, or the disabled and selected states cannot be told apart.

### readable-text-and-contrast: Readable text and contrast (weight 20%, REQUIRED minimum 60%)

Is body text a readable size (about 16 points or more) and does essential text meet accessibility minimums (about 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and meaningful graphics)? You cannot measure exact ratios from pixels. Judge what is visible, say it is an estimate, flag anything borderline, and check that any measured ratios in the rationale are plausible.
Strong (85-100): essential text is clearly well above the minimums and comfortably sized. Adequate (60-84): probably adequate, with one borderline pair. Weak (0-59): essential text visibly fails or is too small.

### legibility-at-small-size: Legibility at small size (weight 10%)

Judge the small-scale preview. Does the hierarchy still read: the route, the departures and the main action?
Strong (85-100): the structure and the main action survive clearly. Adequate (60-84): the structure survives but the main action or one level is strained. Weak (0-59): the hierarchy is lost, or the preview is missing or not a smaller version of the final.

### design-rationale: Design rationale (weight 15%)

Does the rationale explain three real decisions, report the measurements used (target sizes, font sizes, at least two contrast ratios), and match what is visible? Judge reasoning, not writing style.
Strong (85-100): three specific decisions with reasons and measurements that match the work. Adequate (60-84): decisions named, measurements thin or missing. Weak (0-59): generic statements, or claims the work does not show.
