# Create a mini brand sheet

**Assignment objective:** Show that you can turn a short brief into a small, consistent identity: a logo with a lockup, a palette with roles, and two type roles, with one clear usage rule.

**Scenario:** Fernhill Bakery is a small neighbourhood bakery that sells sourdough, pastries and coffee to local families and office workers. The owners describe it as warm, honest and unfussy: "good bread, no airs". They have a name and nothing else. They need a one-page brand sheet they can hand to a printer and a sign maker. The sheet is fictional: nothing you make is used by a real business.

## Requirements
- Show a simple logo or wordmark for Fernhill Bakery, and one lockup (mark plus name) laid out cleanly.
- Show a palette of three to five colours, each labelled with its role (for example primary, neutral, accent).
- Show two type roles (for example headline and body) with the typefaces named.
- Include one clearly drawn usage rule (for example clearspace or a minimum size, or one thing not to do).
- Make the identity fit the brief: warm, honest and unfussy, for families and office workers.
- Explain three design decisions in your rationale, each tied to the brand's position or audience. Give the hex values or sizes you chose.

## Constraints
- Original work only. Any illustration or typeface must be one you are licensed to use.
- Do not copy an existing company's logo or brand guide.
- Use only the information in the brief. Do not invent products, prices or a street address.

## Deliverables
- Brand sheet (required): PNG, JPEG or WebP export of the full one-page sheet.
- Small-scale preview (required): The same sheet exported at about 400 px on its long side, to check that the logo and labels still read.
- Process image: A sketch or an earlier version, if you want to show your process.

## Rubric: Mini brand sheet (pass mark 80%)

### brief-fit: Fit to the brief (weight 20%)

Does the identity express what the brief says (warm, honest, unfussy) for the stated audience (families and office workers), rather than a generic look?
Strong (85-100): colour, type, mark and tone clearly express the brief and the audience, and the rationale shows how.
Adequate (60-84): broadly appropriate, but parts are generic or one choice works against the brief.
Weak (0-59): the identity ignores or contradicts the brief, or could be for any business.

### logo-and-lockup: Logo and lockup (weight 20%, REQUIRED minimum 60%)

Is the mark simple, distinct and legible, and is the lockup (mark plus name) laid out with sensible alignment, proportion and clearspace? Judge only what is visible.
Strong (85-100): a simple, memorable mark; a clean lockup; the name is easy to read; spacing is deliberate.
Adequate (60-84): usable, but spacing, proportion or legibility of the name is uneven.
Weak (0-59): the mark is hard to read or too complicated, the lockup is crowded or inconsistent, or the name is unclear.

### colour-system: Colour system (weight 15%)

Are three to five colours chosen with labelled roles and sensible proportions, with enough contrast between text and its background? You cannot measure exact ratios from pixels: judge what is visible and compare with any values the rationale reports.
Strong (85-100): a small palette with clear roles, a dominant colour, a restrained accent and readable pairings.
Adequate (60-84): a workable palette with unclear roles, or one pairing that is borderline.
Weak (0-59): too many colours, no roles, or essential text that is hard to read.

### type-system: Type system (weight 15%)

Are two type roles named and shown, with clear contrast between headline and body, sensible sizes and a pairing that fits the brand? You cannot identify fonts exactly from pixels; compare with what the rationale states.
Strong (85-100): two roles with clear hierarchy, a fitting pairing and readable sizes.
Adequate (60-84): sound but with weak distinction between roles or a pairing that fits only partly.
Weak (0-59): the roles are unclear, the faces conflict, or text is hard to read.

### legibility-at-small-size: Legibility at small size (weight 15%, REQUIRED minimum 60%)

Judge the small-scale preview. Do the logo, the name and the palette labels still read, and does the sheet's structure survive?
Strong (85-100): the logo and name remain clear and the labels are still usable.
Adequate (60-84): the logo survives but some labels or fine detail are lost.
Weak (0-59): the logo or name becomes unreadable, or the preview is missing or not a smaller version of the final.

### design-rationale: Design rationale (weight 15%)

Does the rationale explain three real decisions, tie each to the brand's position or audience, report the values chosen, and match what is visible? Judge reasoning, not writing style.
Strong (85-100): three specific decisions with reasons that match the work and stated values. Adequate (60-84): decisions named but reasons thin or values missing. Weak (0-59): generic statements or claims the work does not show.
