# Contrast campaign capstone

**Assignment objective:** Design a coordinated three-piece campaign that uses contrast to build hierarchy and stays legible, accessible and consistent across formats.

**Scenario:** Open Studio 2026 is a creative conference on Saturday 14 March at Harbour Hall, Pier 4. Registration is at openstudio.example. The organisers need one coordinated campaign: a printed A3 poster, a square social post, and a small web banner. All three must carry the event name, date, place and one call to action, and they must clearly belong together.

## Requirements
- Deliver three pieces: a poster (portrait), a social square, and a small banner (wide and short).
- Each piece has a clear reading order: event name, then date and place, then the call to action.
- Essential text meets accessibility contrast minimums in every piece, including the banner. Measure at least three pairs and report the ratios in your rationale.
- The pieces are visibly one campaign (shared type, colour logic and contrast approach) but adapted to each format, not copied.
- Use no more than two typefaces across the campaign.
- Explain three design decisions in your rationale, each tied to contrast.

## Constraints
- Original work only. Any photograph or illustration must be one you are licensed to use.
- Do not reuse your module assessment poster unchanged. This campaign must be a new design.
- Include only the four pieces of event information. Do not invent extra details.

## Deliverables
- Poster (required): Portrait poster, exported as PNG, JPEG or WebP.
- Social square (required): Square social post, exported as PNG, JPEG or WebP.
- Web banner (required): Wide, short banner at roughly 728 by 90 or similar, exported as PNG, JPEG or WebP.
- Process image: A sketch or earlier version, if you want to show your process.

## Rubric: Contrast campaign capstone (pass mark 85%)

### campaign-hierarchy: Information hierarchy (weight 20%)

In each piece, does contrast make the reading order obvious (what the event is, when and where, what to do)?
Strong (85-100): all three pieces have an immediate, correct reading order that adapts to its format.
Adequate (60-84): the order is clear in most pieces but one competes or is unclear.
Weak (0-59): several elements have similar emphasis, or the main message is not the most prominent.

### contrast: Contrast (value and colour) (weight 15%, REQUIRED minimum 60%)

Is value and colour contrast strong, selective and deliberate across the pieces, separating figure from ground and groups from each other?
Strong (85-100): contrast is used purposefully in every piece without everything shouting.
Adequate (60-84): mostly effective with uneven use or one weak piece.
Weak (0-59): contrast is too uniform to separate anything, or too weak to read.

### accessible-contrast: Accessible contrast (weight 15%, REQUIRED minimum 60%)

Does essential text meet accessibility minimums (about 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and meaningful graphics)? You cannot measure exact ratios from pixels. Judge what is visible, say it is an estimate, and flag anything borderline for measuring. If the rationale reports measured ratios, check that they are plausible given what you see.
Strong (85-100): all essential text is clearly well above the minimums in every piece, including the small banner.
Adequate (60-84): essential text is probably adequate, but one pair is borderline or unverified.
Weak (0-59): essential text visibly fails or is likely to fail.

### legibility-across-formats: Legibility across formats (weight 15%)

Judge the smallest piece. Do the event name, date, place and call to action stay readable and in order at that size?
Strong (85-100): the hierarchy and all essential text survive in the smallest piece.
Adequate (60-84): survives with one strained item.
Weak (0-59): essential text becomes unreadable or lost in the smallest piece.

### consistency-across-pieces: Consistency across pieces (weight 15%, REQUIRED minimum 60%)

Do the pieces read as one coordinated campaign: shared type system, colour logic, contrast approach and spacing, adapted rather than copied to each format?
Strong (85-100): clearly one system, thoughtfully adapted to each format.
Adequate (60-84): related, with some drift in type, colour or hierarchy.
Weak (0-59): the pieces look unrelated, or are identical crops with no adaptation.

### typography: Typography (weight 10%)

Intentional choice and use of type: hierarchy through size and weight, line length and spacing that support reading, faces that do not conflict, used consistently across pieces.
Strong (85-100): a clear, purposeful type system. Adequate (60-84): sound with inconsistencies. Weak (0-59): arbitrary or conflicting type that harms reading.

### design-rationale: Design rationale (weight 10%)

Does the rationale explain three real decisions across the campaign, tie them to contrast and to the audience, and match what is visible? Judge reasoning, not writing style.
Strong (85-100): three specific decisions with reasons that match the work. Adequate (60-84): decisions named, reasons thin. Weak (0-59): generic statements or claims the work does not show.
